Questions Everyone Keeps Asking

How much does an Anchorage personal injury attorney cost?

Almost every personal injury attorney in Anchorage works on a contingency fee basis, which means you pay nothing upfront and the attorney takes a percentage of your settlement or court award. In Alaska, that typically runs between 33% and 40% depending on the complexity of the case and whether it goes to trial. If you don't win, you owe nothing — though you should always ask about costs versus fees and whether case expenses like filing fees or expert witnesses come out of your portion.

How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Alaska?

Alaska's statute of limitations for most personal injury claims is two years from the date of the injury. That sounds like a lot of time, but evidence degrades, witnesses disappear, and insurance companies use delay as a tactic — so earlier is almost always better. There are exceptions that can shorten this window, including claims against government entities, so don't assume you have the full two years without confirming with an attorney.

What should I do immediately after a car accident in Anchorage?

Don't give a recorded statement to the insurance company — not your own, not the other driver's — before talking to an attorney. Beyond that: get medical attention even if you feel okay (symptoms from soft tissue injuries often show up 24 to 72 hours later), document everything you can at the scene, and preserve any evidence including photos, witness contact information, and dashcam footage. The decisions you make in the first 48 to 72 hours can have significant effects on your case value.

Can I still recover compensation if I was partially at fault for the accident?

Yes, Alaska follows a modified comparative negligence rule. As long as you are less than 50% at fault for the accident, you can still recover damages — but your compensation will be reduced by your percentage of fault. Insurance companies routinely try to inflate your share of the fault to reduce their payout, which is one of many reasons having an attorney during negotiations matters.

How do I know if my personal injury case is worth pursuing?

The honest answer is that you often can't fully evaluate this without legal expertise, which is why most reputable personal injury attorneys offer free initial consultations. As a rough guide, cases with documented medical treatment, clear liability, and measurable economic losses like missed wages tend to be the most straightforward to pursue. But even cases that seem complicated on the surface can have significant value, and cases that look simple can have hidden challenges — talk to an attorney before deciding.

What's the difference between settling and going to trial in a personal injury case?

The vast majority of personal injury cases — somewhere in the range of 95% — settle before trial. Settling is faster, more certain, and avoids the cost and stress of litigation. But settling for the wrong number is a permanent decision; once you sign a release, it's over. The value of having an attorney who is genuinely willing and able to go to trial is that insurance companies know it — and they negotiate very differently with attorneys who have real trial track records than with those who almost always settle.

Do I need an attorney if the insurance company already made me an offer?

Especially then. First settlement offers from insurance companies are almost never their best offers, and they're often made quickly and urgently specifically to close the case before you understand what your injuries will actually cost you long-term. Future medical expenses, lost earning capacity, and pain and suffering are all components of a full claim that rarely get adequately reflected in an initial offer. A quick free consultation with a personal injury attorney before you sign anything costs you nothing and could be worth a significant amount.

How long does a personal injury case take to resolve in Alaska?

It varies widely. A clear-cut car accident case with documented injuries and cooperative insurers might resolve in four to eight months. Cases involving disputed liability, serious injuries, multiple parties, or defendants who fight hard can take two years or longer. Your attorney should be able to give you a realistic range after reviewing the specifics of your situation, and they should communicate proactively throughout the process so you're never in the dark about where things stand.

What if I can't afford to miss work for attorney appointments?

Most personal injury attorneys in Anchorage are flexible about consultation scheduling and, for ongoing case work, can handle significant portions of the communication remotely. This is worth asking about directly during your first call. The contingency fee structure also means the financial barrier to starting a case is low — you don't write a check until and unless you win.

Is Alaska a no-fault insurance state?

No. Alaska is an at-fault state, which means the driver who caused the accident is responsible for damages. You can file a claim directly against the at-fault driver's liability insurance, and you're not required to go through your own insurer first. This is actually a simpler framework in many respects than no-fault states, but it also means proving fault is central to your case — another reason legal representation during negotiations with the at-fault driver's insurer makes a material difference.
